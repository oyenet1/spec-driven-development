## 1. Sabri Suby (*Sell Like Crazy*)

### Post 1 (The 3% Rule)
> 90% of businesses are screaming "BUY MY STUFF!" to the 3% of people who want to buy today.  
> 
> That's why your ad costs are crazy high.  
> 
> The real money is in teaching and helping the other 97%. When they're ready to buy, who do you think they'll go to?  
> 
> — **Sabri Suby**  
> `#MarketingTips #BusinessGrowth #SalesSecrets`

---

### Post 2 (Sell Like a Doctor)
> A doctor who writes a prescription without asking where it hurts is doing malpractice.  
> 
> So why do salespeople vomit features and talk non-stop before knowing the customer's real problem?  
> 
> Stop pitching. Start diagnosing.  
> 
> — **Sabri Suby**  
> `#SalesTips #ClosingDeals #Consulting`

---

### Post 3 (The Godfather Offer)
> If your offer doesn't make your customer feel crazy for saying no, your offer is too weak.  
> 
> Make it so good, pack it with so much value, and remove all risk with a money-back guarantee so that saying "YES" is a no-brainer.  
> 
> — **Sabri Suby**  
> `#OffersThatSell #Entrepreneurship #DirectResponse`

---

### Post 4 (Plain Text Emails)
> Stop sending fancy, colorful corporate emails with 10 buttons and giant banners.  
> 
> Your friends send you simple plain-text emails. That's why you open them.  
> 
> Write like a human talking to a friend. Watch your sales double.  
> 
> — **Sabri Suby**  
> `#EmailMarketing #Copywriting #SalesHacks`

---

### Post 5 (The 9-Word Email)
> The highest-converting email in marketing history is only 9 words long:  
> 
> *"Are you still looking for help with [goal]?"*  
> 
> Send this on Sunday night to every lead who stopped replying. You'll be shocked at how many people respond.  
> 
> — **Sabri Suby**  
> `#EmailTips #LeadGeneration #Sales`

### Post 10 (Facts vs Stories)
> Facts tell, but stories sell.  
> 
> People will forget your numbers and feature list by tomorrow morning.  
> But they will remember a story about how someone just like them struggled, found a way out, and won.  
> 
> — **Paul Smith**  
> `#Storytelling #SalesSkills #Influence`

---

### Post 11 (No Struggle = No Story)

> Tell them about the late nights, the fear, and the mistakes. That is where trust is built.  
> 
> — **Paul Smith**  
> `#Authenticity #StorytellingSales #Leadership`

> — **Paul Smith**

## 5. Dan Lok (*Influence!*, *Unlock It*, *F.U. Money*)

> If you want people to say yes, always give them a reason.  
> 
> Saying *"We only have 5 spots left BECAUSE our team works with you 1-on-1"* makes people believe you immediately.  
> Never state a rule without saying why.  
> 
> — **Dan Lok**  
> `#Persuasion #PsychologyOfSelling #Influence`

---

### Post 18 (Honesty Sells)
> Want people to believe everything good about your product?  
> 
> Tell them one small thing that is tough about it first.  
> *"Look, this requires 3 hours of focus every week, it's not magic."*  
> Instant trust unlocked.  
> 
> — **Dan Lok**  
> `#Copywriting #HonestyInBusiness #SalesClosing`

---

### Post 19 (Stop Chasing Clients)
> When you look desperate for a sale, the customer runs away.  
> 
> When you act calm and say, *"I'm not sure if our program is the right fit for you yet,"* they start trying to impress you.  
> Stop selling, and let them buy.  
> 
> — **Dan Lok**  
> `#HighTicketSales #Positioning #Negotiation`

## 6. Brian Tracy (*The Psychology of Selling* & *Negotiation*)

### Post 20 (The 3% Edge)
> In horse racing, the horse that wins by just a nose gets 10 times more prize money—even though it was only 1% faster.  
> 
> You don't need to be twice as good as other sellers.  
> If you are just 3% better at listening and asking questions, your income can multiply tenfold.  
> 
> — **Brian Tracy**  
> `#TheWinningEdge #SalesMastery #SuccessMindset`

---

### Post 21 (Walk Away Power)
> In any deal or negotiation, the person who is most willing to walk away holds all the power.  
> 
> Always have other options so you never feel desperate.  
> 
> — **Brian Tracy**  
> `#Negotiation #DealMaking #Confidence`

---

### Post 22 (Which One Do You Want?)
> Never ask a customer: *"Do you want to buy this?"*  
> 
> Ask: *"Would you prefer the black or blue one?"* Or *"Do you want morning or afternoon delivery?"*  
> Give them a choice between two good options.  
> 
> — **Brian Tracy**  
> `#SalesClosing #PsychologyOfSelling #Tips`
